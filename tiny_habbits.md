## Question 1 : In this video, what was the most interesting story or idea for you?

The most interesting idea for me was that we should start with very small habits instead of trying to make big changes at once. For example, doing just two push-ups can slowly help us build a regular exercise habit. I liked this idea because small steps feel easy and are easier to continue.

## Question 2 : How can you use B = MAP to make making new habits easier? What are M, A and P.

I can use B = MAP by making the habit simple and easy to do. M means Motivation, A means Ability, and P means Prompt. For example, if I want to read daily, I can keep a book near my bed and read one page before sleeping.

## Question 3 : Why it is important to "Shine" or Celebrate after each successful completion of habit? 

Celebrating after completing a habit helps our brain connect that action with a positive feeling. When we feel happy or proud, we are more likely to repeat the habit again. Even a small celebration like saying “Good job” to yourself can help make the habit stronger.

## Question 5 : What is the book's perspective about Identity?

The book explains that habits are connected to our identity. Instead of only focusing on what we want to achieve, we should focus on the kind of person we want to become. For example, instead of saying “I want to read more,” we can think, “I am a person who reads every day.”

## Question 6 : Write about the book's perspective on how to make a habit easier to do?

The book says that we can make a habit easier by starting with a very small step. Instead of trying to do a big task, we should make it simple enough to do without much effort. For example, if I want to start reading every day, I can begin by reading just one page.

## Question 7 : Write about the book's perspective on how to make a habit harder to do?

The book says we can reduce a bad habit by making it difficult to do. For example, if I want to use my phone less, I can keep it away while studying.
